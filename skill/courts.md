## 1. The 50% ceiling on SC+ST+OBC (vertical) — REAL, binding, Supreme Court

**K. Krishna Murthy (Dr.) & Ors. v. Union of India & Anr., (2010) 7 SCC 202, Constitution Bench, decided 11 May 2010** — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1451338/
- Para 37: "the upper ceiling of 50% (quantitative limitation) with respect to vertical reservations in favour of SC/ST/OBCs should not be breached." Same para on women: "the horizontal reservations in favour of women are meant to intersect with the vertical reservations in favour of SC/ST/OBC … seats earmarked for women belonging to the general category are not accounted for if one has to gauge whether the upper ceiling of 50% has been breached."
- Para 39: "vertical reservations in favour of SC/ST/OBCs cannot exceed the upper limit of 50% when taken together."
- Para 48(iv) (conclusions): "The upper ceiling of 50% vertical reservations in favour of SC/ST/OBCs should not be breached in the context of local self-government. Exceptions can only be made in order to safeguard the interests of the Scheduled Tribes in the matter of their representation in panchayats located in the Scheduled Areas."
- What it means for us: SC + ST + OBC seats/offices in a body ≤ 50% of the total, outside Scheduled Areas. **Women are horizontal — women's seats/tags do not count toward this ceiling and the ceiling says nothing about how many women's seats there are.**

**Vikas Kishanrao Gawali v. State of Maharashtra, (2021) 6 SCC 73, decided 4 March 2021** — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/178057376/
- Para 67 (quoting Krishna Murthy): "the vertical reservations in favour of SCs/STs/OBCs cannot exceed the upper limit of 50% when taken together."
- Para 82(iv): "The upper ceiling of 50% vertical reservations in favour of SCs/STs/OBCs should not be breached in the context of local self-government."
- The "triple test" for OBC reservation in local bodies (set out in the body of the judgment restating Krishna Murthy — cite Gawali paras 6–14 for the test itself; para 82 holds the conclusions; do NOT attribute the test to para 82(iv), which is only the 50% line): (i) a dedicated commission's empirical inquiry into backwardness in local bodies; (ii) the proportion of reservation specified body-wise in the light of the commission's recommendations; (iii) the aggregate SC+ST+OBC not exceeding 50%.
- Para 28 (remedy): the impugned notifications were "quashed and set aside to the extent of providing reservation of seats … for OBCs" beyond the ceiling, those seats "to be filled by general/open category candidates." → **the remedy for an excess is trimming to open, not voiding the election.**
- Women's reservation appears only as background statute (Maharashtra s.12(2)(d) "one-half … for women"), not analysed.

**Sangram Govindrao Patil v. State of Maharashtra, W.P.(C) 806/2026 (SC, notice 17 July 2026)** — pending; petition seeks that reservations beyond the 50% ceiling be treated as open seats. No ruling on merits yet.

## 2. Women's reservation quantum / rounding — NO ruling requires "not more than half / round down"

Search run 16 Aug 2026 (5 angles: Rajasthan HC via IndianKanoon and court portals, Supreme Court, other High Courts, SEC/PRD/DLB circulars and news in Hindi and English), 9 candidate judgments verified against text: **none holds that women's total in panchayats may not exceed one-half or that an odd total rounds down.** The nearest things, each of which an office might be half-remembering:

**Manak Chand & Ors. v. State of Rajasthan & Ors. (with Mohd. Kaleem), Rajasthan HC Jaipur DB, DB CWP 12935/2009, decided 5 March 2010** — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/102000227/
- About the **Rajasthan Municipalities Act** (urban bodies), s.6(6)/(7) raising women from one-third to one-half, and s.21A (age bar). Quotes: "The Hon'ble Supreme Court, while considering the matter in the case of Indra Sawhney (supra) put a rider that reservation should not exceed to 50% in ordinary case." "If for example, total proportionate population ratio between male and female is 55% and 45% respectively in any State then reservation of 50% seats for women candidates in proportion to population would be more than 50%." "The respondents have not come with any material to justify amendment… They have not even furnished the details of the proportionate population between male and female in the State of Rajasthan." Also records the State's argument: "women reservation is horizontal, thus … while counting total vertical reservation horizontal reservation is not counted."
- Operative: s.21A struck down; the 50%-women provision read down — "If the amended provisions are interpreted as indicated above [proportionate to female population], then the respondents will undertake their exercise accordingly and, in that, even amended is not struck down, otherwise, it is held to be ultra vires and are accordingly struck down."
- Effect in practice: Rajasthan urban bodies have run women's reservation at ⅓ since (DLB 2026 tables: ⅓ per category, rounded to nearest). **It is not a panchayat ruling and it says nothing about rounding a fraction.** Two months later the Supreme Court in K. Krishna Murthy treated women's reservation as horizontal and outside the 50% count; Rajasthan PRIs have run at 50% women since 2010.

**Ashok Kumar Malpani v. State of M.P., MP HC (Dipak Misra & R.K. Gupta JJ), 22 Oct 2009, AIR 2010 MP 64** — challenge to MP municipal women's 50%; the State's return: reservation "is up to the extent of 50% and not beyond the said percentage" — an argument about quantum in MP municipalities, not a rounding rule and not Rajasthan.

**Karnataka Panchayat Raj Act amendment (legislative fix, secondary source)** — inserted words to the effect "subject to an overall limit of one half of the total number rounding off to the next number" — i.e. rounds UP, same direction as Rajasthan's s.16 Explanation.

**Bottom line for the desk:** the *text* (s.15(6)/s.16(5) "not less than one-half"; s.16 Explanation "half or more … increased to the next higher number"; r.6(2), r.7(9) "÷ 2") points UP for an odd total; the *state's practice* is DOWN (13.08.2026 Pramukh draw: 20 women of 41; department direction to districts, Aug 2026). No court has resolved it. See faq.md Q1–Q3 for how to answer and what to record.

## 3. Lottery procedure and redraws — Rajasthan HC

**Virender Singh v. State of Rajasthan, RHC 26.02.2020 (batch of writs, 2020 panchayat lotteries)** — redraws after population/notification corrections were upheld: "it was necessary to undertake exercise for redrawing the lotteries for all the Gram Panchayats"; individual challenges to draws "for lack of any substance are rejected." → a substantive change in the inputs (population, delimitation) requires a fresh draw with fresh notice; a properly conducted lottery is not lightly disturbed.

**Jai Singh v. State of Rajasthan, RHC DB 13.12.2019** — corrections that are "purely rectifying the typographical errors" do not reopen the process; substantive changes do.

## 4. Constitutional anchors
- Art. 243D(1)-(3) (panchayats) / 243T(1)-(3) (municipalities): SC/ST seats "in proportion to population"; not less than one-third for women (Rajasthan raised to one-half for PRIs by state law, 2008; for ULBs the 2009 one-half was read down in Manak Chand).
- Art. 243D(4)/243T(4): offices of chairpersons reserved as the Legislature provides, allotted by rotation.
- Art. 243D(6): enabling provision for backward-class reservation — subject to the Krishna Murthy triple test.
